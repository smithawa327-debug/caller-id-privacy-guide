# How Caller ID Works

Caller ID is a technology that displays information about an incoming call before the recipient answers it. Depending on the phone, mobile network, and caller ID service being used, this information may include a phone number, a name, or a business identity.

## What Happens When You Receive a Call?

When someone makes a phone call, the telephone network sends the caller's phone number along with signaling information used to establish the call. The receiving network or device can then use this information to display the incoming number.

A caller ID service may also compare the number with available contact information or a caller identification database to provide additional details.

## Where Does the Caller Name Come From?

The name displayed for a phone number can come from different sources, including:

* Your personal contacts
* Mobile carrier information
* Publicly available business information
* Third-party caller identification databases
* Information reported or contributed by users

Because different services may use different data sources, the name shown for the same phone number can sometimes vary between applications.

## Why Can Caller ID Information Be Incorrect?

Caller ID information is not always accurate. A phone number may have been reassigned to a new user, a database may contain outdated information, or the information may have been incorrectly reported.

In some cases, scammers may also use techniques that make an incoming call appear to come from a different number. This is commonly known as caller ID spoofing.

## Caller ID and Spam Detection

Some caller identification applications and phone services attempt to identify suspicious or unwanted calls. They may use information such as previously reported numbers, known spam patterns, or other available signals.

However, spam detection is not perfect. A legitimate number may occasionally be flagged incorrectly, while a new or previously unknown spam number may not be detected.

## Protecting Yourself

Caller ID information should be treated as helpful information rather than absolute proof of who is calling. If a caller requests sensitive information, money, passwords, or verification codes, it is important to verify their identity through an official and trusted contact method.

## Conclusion

Caller ID technology helps users identify incoming calls and can provide additional information about unknown numbers. However, displayed information may be incomplete or inaccurate, so users should remain cautious and independently verify suspicious calls.
